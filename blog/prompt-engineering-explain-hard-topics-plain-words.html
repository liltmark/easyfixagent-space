Prompt Engineering: Get AI to Explain Hard Topics in Plain Words

November 16, 2026 · Prompt Engineering · 5 min read
Written & tested by Muhammad Mudassir YaseenFounder, EasyFix Agent, every guide on this site is tested by hand before it is published.
🗓 Last updated: November 2026

A good explanation feels simple, but simple explanations are hard to write, and harder still to ask for. Most people type a topic into a chatbot and accept whatever comes back, even when the answer is full of jargon they do not understand. The problem is rarely the tool. It is the prompt. A clear prompt tells the AI who is asking, how much they already know, and how plain the answer should be. This guide shows how to prompt for explanations that actually make sense, so hard topics become clear instead of staying impressive and useless.

1. Name your level before you ask the question

An explanation for a beginner looks nothing like an explanation for an expert, so tell the AI where you stand. Start with one sentence about your level, such as that you are new to the topic, or that you know the basics but not the details. That single line changes everything that follows, because the AI stops guessing and starts aiming at the right reader.

Be honest about the gaps. If you do not know a key term, say so in the prompt, and ask for the term to be explained first. Pretending to know more than you do only produces answers you cannot follow. A prompt that admits the starting point gets an answer that starts from the right place.

2. Ask for plain words, not short words

Short answers are not the same as clear answers. Asking for a short explanation often produces a summary that skips the part you needed most. Ask instead for plain words: everyday language, no jargon unless it is explained, and one idea per sentence. That instruction forces the AI to slow down and build the idea piece by piece, which is what understanding actually needs.

Name the words to avoid when you know them. If a topic is full of technical terms that confuse you, list two or three of them in the prompt and ask for plain replacements. The AI will still use the correct term where it matters, but it will explain it the first time it appears, and that small change makes the whole answer easier to follow.

3. Ask for one example from real life

An abstract idea becomes concrete the moment it meets a real example. Ask the AI to explain the idea with one example from everyday life, such as cooking, driving, or running a small shop. A single good example does more work than three paragraphs of definition, because it gives your mind something to hold on to while the idea settles.

Check whether the example actually fits. If the example feels forced or confusing, say so and ask for a different one. Our guide to persona and role prompts shows the same idea from another angle: giving the AI a clear role produces answers that fit the reader better.

4. Build the explanation in layers

Hard topics rarely yield to a single question. Ask for the simplest version first, then ask a follow up that adds one layer of detail. The first answer gives you the shape of the idea. The second answer fills in the parts that matter. The third answer handles the exceptions. Each layer lands because the one before it is already clear in your mind.

Write down what each layer taught you before you ask for the next one. A short note in your own words shows whether you really understood the last step, and it gives you exact words to quote when the next prompt needs context. If you cannot restate the idea simply, ask the AI to explain that one part again, more slowly.

5. Ask the AI to test you, not just tell you

Reading an explanation feels like learning, but it is only the first half. Ask the AI to quiz you with three short questions after each explanation, and answer them without looking back at the text.

When you get a question wrong, ask for the explanation of that one point again, in different words. A second explanation from a new angle often unlocks what the first one missed. Our guide to using an AI chatbot as a study tutor covers this loop in more detail, and it works for any subject, not just schoolwork.

6. Keep a file of explanations that worked

Good explanations are worth keeping. Save the ones that finally made a topic click, with the exact prompt you used, so you can reuse the same approach on the next hard topic. Over time that file becomes a personal guide to how you learn best, and it saves you from starting each new topic with a blank prompt.

Review the file once a month and look for patterns. You may find that examples from everyday life help you most, or that step by step layers work better than one long answer. Those patterns are your own prompting style, and they make every future explanation better. For the basics of writing clear prompts, our prompt engineering basics guide is the place to start.

Bottom line: Name your level, ask for plain words, demand one real example, build the idea in layers, let the AI test you, and keep the explanations that worked. A clear explanation is not a gift from the tool. It is the result of a prompt that says exactly what you need.

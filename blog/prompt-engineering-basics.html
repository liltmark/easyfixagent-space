Prompt Engineering Basics: Talk to AI Like a Pro

October 18, 2026 · Guides · 6 min read

Everyone uses AI now. The people who get great results are not smarter — they just prompt better. Here are the fundamentals.

1. Be specific

Bad: "Write about dogs." Good: "Write a 200-word beginner guide to choosing a family dog breed, friendly tone, bullet list of 5 breeds." Specificity is the whole game.

2. Give context

AI has no idea who you are. Tell it: your goal, your audience, your constraints. Two sentences of context transform the output.

3. Show, don't just tell

Paste an example of what "good" looks like. AI imitates examples far better than it follows abstract instructions.

4. Break big tasks down

Instead of "write my business plan", do it in steps: outline first, then expand each section, then polish. Each step gets full attention.

5. Iterate

Treat the first answer as a draft. "Make it shorter", "more formal", "add examples" — three rounds of feedback beat one perfect prompt.

Common mistakes

  • Too vague: "help me with marketing" helps nobody.
  • Too much at once: five requests in one prompt get five shallow answers.
  • No constraints: without length, tone and format, you get generic filler.
  • Trusting blindly: always verify facts that matter.

A template you can reuse

Act as [role]. I need [deliverable] for [audience]. Context: [2-3 sentences]. Format: [bullets/table/steps]. Keep it [tone] and under [length].

Fill in the brackets and you will outperform 90% of users immediately.

Turn a Blog Post into a Short Video with AI: A Practical Workflow

November 1, 2026 · AI Video Generators · 5 min read
Written & tested by Muhammad Mudassir YaseenFounder, EasyFix Agent — every guide on this site is tested by hand before it is published.
🗓 Last updated: November 2026

A good blog post contains five or six short videos hiding inside it. Each section makes one point, gives one example, and ends with one takeaway, which is exactly the shape of a short vertical video. Instead of writing video scripts from scratch, you can mine posts you already published, and the result is faster to make and easier to trust, because the content is already written and checked.

1. Pick one idea, not the whole post

The first mistake is trying to film the entire post. A short video holds one idea, not five. Read the post and pick the section with the strongest single point: the surprising fact, the common mistake, or the step that changes the result. That section becomes the video, and the rest of the post becomes future videos.

Test the idea with one sentence. If you cannot state the video point in one sentence, the idea is still too broad. Narrow it until the sentence is obvious, then build everything around that sentence. Viewers remember one clear point; they forget five rushed ones.

2. Rewrite the section as a spoken script

Written text and spoken text are different languages. A paragraph that reads well can sound stiff aloud, because sentences are longer, words are more formal, and the rhythm is flat. Rewrite the section as speech: short sentences, plain words, and a conversational opening that states the point within three seconds.

Read the script aloud before you generate anything. If you stumble, the script needs fixing, not the voice tool. Aim for 120 to 150 words for a one minute video. Our guide to a solid video script workflow covers this rewrite step in detail.

3. Choose visuals that show, not decorate

For each sentence of the script, decide what the viewer should see. A step should show the action, a comparison should show both sides, and a warning should show the mistake happening. Visuals that merely decorate, such as random stock footage behind a voiceover, lose attention fast.

Generate or select one visual per idea, not per sentence. Changing the picture every three seconds feels frantic, while one steady visual per idea feels calm and clear. Keep the style consistent across the video so the clips feel like one piece rather than a slideshow of unrelated images.

4. Add captions and check them word by word

Most short videos are watched without sound at first, so captions are not optional. Generate captions automatically, then read every word against the script. Automatic captions mishear names, numbers and technical terms, and one wrong word in a caption can reverse the meaning of a sentence.

Style the captions for readability: large text, high contrast, and no more than two lines on screen at once. Our guide to captions and subtitles covers the workflow, and it is worth following exactly, because caption errors are the most common quality complaint on short videos.

5. Keep the format right for each platform

A vertical video, a square video and a wide video are different products, not the same file resized. Vertical suits short feeds, square suits some social feeds, and wide suits video platforms. Generate or frame your visuals for the platform you will publish on, and check that faces and text survive the crop on each one.

Also respect length norms. Short feeds reward videos under sixty seconds with a fast opening, while longer platforms tolerate a slower build. Our guide to short-form social videos explains how pacing changes by platform.

6. Link back to the post and reuse the video

Publish the video with a link or a clear mention of the full post. Viewers who want depth can read the original, and readers of the post can watch the summary. One piece of work now serves two audiences, and each format sends traffic to the other.

Then reuse the video itself. Cut a shorter version for stories, use the opening frame as a thumbnail image, and turn the script into a caption for a text post. A single blog section can feed a week of content when you plan the reuse before you start generating. Keep a simple log of which sections you have already turned into videos, so you do not repeat a section by accident and so you can see which topics your audience responds to most strongly.

Bottom line: Bottom line: One section, one spoken script, visuals that show the point, checked captions, and a link back to the post. That is the whole workflow.

Write a Press Release with AI: News, Quotes and the Human Edit

November 26, 2026 · AI Writing Tools · 5 min read
Written & tested by Muhammad Mudassir YaseenFounder, EasyFix Agent, every guide on this site is tested by hand before it is published.
🗓 Last updated: November 2026

A press release is a short news document with one job: to tell a journalist, in under a minute, why a story matters and what happened. To write a press release with AI is to fix the structure first, and let the tool draft the sentences around a clear news angle. The tool is good at shape, order, and plain language, but it cannot know what is actually new, so the news must come from you. This guide shows how to give the tool the right facts, shape the draft, and edit it into a release an editor can use.

1. Find the news before you write a word

A press release needs one clear news point, and that point must be true and checkable. A new product, a new service, a survey result, a milestone, or an event are all real news. A company being pleased, excited, or proud is not news, and no editor will print it. Write the news in one sentence before you open the tool, and if you cannot write that sentence, you do not have a release yet.

Check the sentence against a simple test: would a stranger care, and could a journalist check it in five minutes. If the answer to either question is no, find a stronger angle or wait until you have one.

2. Write a press release with AI from a clear outline

Give the tool a short brief, not a vague request. List the news in one line, the who, what, when, and where, one quote from a named person, and the contact details for follow up questions. Then ask for a release under 400 words with a headline, a first paragraph that carries the news, two short body paragraphs, one quote, and a short boilerplate about the company.

Read the draft for structure first and style second. The headline should carry the news, not the company name. The first paragraph should answer who, what, when, and where in two sentences. If the draft opens with background or praise, ask the tool to move the news to the top. Our guide to structuring a business plan uses the same rule of facts first and background last, and it works for a press release too.

3. Quotes that sound like a person said them

A good quote adds a human view that the facts alone cannot carry, such as why this launch matters, what problem it solves, or what happens next. A bad quote repeats the facts in fancier words, and editors cut those first. Write the quote yourself in plain speech, then ask the tool to tidy the grammar without changing the meaning, and read it aloud to check it sounds like something a real person would say.

Never invent a quote, and never put words in the mouth of a named person who did not approve them. If the named person has not seen the quote, send it to them and get a yes in writing before the release goes out. A quote that a real person will stand behind is worth more than three polished quotes nobody will own.

4. Keep the facts tight and check every number

Every number in a release must be exact, because journalists will check it, and a wrong number will kill the story and your credibility with it. Check every date, price, percentage, and name against the source document, not against your memory, and ask the tool to list every number in the draft so you can check them one by one. If a number is an estimate, say so in plain words.

Cut any claim you cannot prove with a document, a link, or a named source. Words like leading, best, first, and largest are claims, and an editor will ask for proof, so keep them out unless you can show the evidence. Our guide to landing page copy that converts makes the same point about proof, because readers trust specific facts far more than big adjectives.

5. Format the release so an editor can use it fast

Put the headline at the top, then the city and date, then the news paragraph, then the body, then the quote, then the boilerplate, then the contact details. Keep paragraphs short, keep the whole release on one page if you can, and put the most important link in the first three lines, because many editors will not scroll further.

Send the release as plain text in the body of the email, not as an attachment nobody will open, and put the news in the subject line, not the words press release. A subject line that carries the news gets opened, while a subject line that only says press release gets deleted. Our guide to cold outreach emails covers the same subject line habit, and it applies to press releases just as well.

6. The human edit that makes it safe to send

Read the draft once for tone, once for facts, and once aloud, and fix a different problem each time. The tone read should cut jargon and praise. The facts read should check every name, date, and number. The read aloud should catch sentences that are too long, words that repeat, and quotes that sound fake. Three short passes beat one long stare, because each pass has a single job.

Ask one other person to read the release before you send it, ideally someone outside your team, and ask them one question: what is the news here. If they cannot answer in one sentence, the release is not ready. Our guide to a better newsletter structure shows the same habit of one clear point per message, and it keeps a press release readable too.

Bottom line: To write a press release with AI is to find the news first, brief the tool with facts, shape the draft around the news, write quotes a real person will own, check every number, and finish with three short human edits. The tool gives you speed, and the edit gives you trust.

AI Images for Blog Headers: Featured Images That Earn the Click

November 15, 2026 · AI Image Generators · 5 min read
Written & tested by Muhammad Mudassir YaseenFounder, EasyFix Agent, every guide on this site is tested by hand before it is published.
🗓 Last updated: November 2026

A blog header is the first thing a reader sees, and it decides in a second whether the article looks worth opening. A strong header supports the headline, sets the mood of the piece, and tells the reader what kind of article follows. A weak header does the opposite: it confuses the topic, clashes with the text, or looks like a random stock photo that has nothing to do with the words below it. AI image generators can make a fitting header for every post in minutes, but only when you guide them with care. This guide shows how to make blog header images with AI, so each one earns the click instead of wasting it.

1. Match the image to the promise of the headline

Read your headline before you write the image prompt, and ask what picture the headline promises. A headline about a calm morning routine promises soft light and a quiet scene, while a headline about a common mistake promises a clear warning sign or a before and after contrast.

Write the prompt from the headline, not from a generic idea of a nice picture. Name the subject, the setting, and the feeling in one short sentence, and leave out details that have nothing to do with the article. A prompt that tries to include everything produces a crowded image that says nothing, while a prompt built on one clear idea produces a header that supports the words. Our guide to AI image prompt mistakes that ruin good pictures shows how a short focused prompt beats a long vague one every time.

2. Pick one clear focal point

A header works when the eye lands on one thing first, then moves across the rest of the image. Choose a single subject for that first landing point, such as a person, a product, or a strong shape, and keep everything else quieter.

Place the focal point off centre, so the image has room to breathe on one side. That empty side is useful: it gives the headline space when the image appears in a feed, and it stops the picture from fighting the text for attention. Generate three or four versions with the same prompt, and pick the one where the focal point is clearest, not the one with the most detail.

3. Size the image for the place it will appear

A header that looks perfect on a desktop can crop badly on a phone, so size it for the narrowest place it will appear, not the widest. Most blog themes show the header at a wide ratio on desktop and a taller crop on mobile, which means the edges and the top of the image often disappear. Keep the important parts near the centre, and leave a margin around the subject that a crop can safely cut.

Export the image at the size your theme actually uses, and check the file size before you upload it. Resize and compress the image to the theme width, and test it on a phone and a laptop before you publish. Our guide to AI image styles explained can help you pick a style that stays clear when the image is cropped small.

4. Keep one style across the whole blog

A blog feels professional when its headers share one look, and it feels random when every post uses a different style. Pick one style for your headers, such as soft photography, flat illustration, or warm 3D, and use it for every new post. A consistent style tells the reader that the blog is one publication with one voice, not a collection of unrelated posts.

Save the exact prompt words that define your style, including the colour palette and the lighting, and reuse them for every header. Add the topic to the same base prompt, so each image is different in subject and identical in feel. Our guide to a consistent brand visual style with AI images shows how to build that base prompt once and reuse it well.

5. Leave room for the headline and the text

Many themes place the headline over the header image, so an image with text baked into it will clash with the real headline. Ask the image generator for a clean image with no words, no logos, and no fake text on signs or screens. AI tools still render text badly, and a header with garbled letters looks careless even when the rest of the article is excellent.

Check the contrast where the headline will sit. If the headline appears over a bright part of the image, the words become hard to read, and the reader moves on. Choose images with a calm area at the top or on one side, where text can sit comfortably, and test the header with the real headline in place before you publish.

6. Test the header on a phone before you publish

Most readers will meet your header on a phone, in a feed, at a small size, so test it there before you publish. Open the article on your own phone, scroll the feed as a stranger would, and ask whether the header still reads clearly: one focal point, one feeling, and no clutter. If the image turns into a blur of colour at small size, pick a simpler version.

Check the header in the places where readers share it as well, such as the social preview card and the search result. A header that crops well in all three places is worth keeping, while one that works only on desktop is not.

Bottom line: Match the image to the promise of the headline, pick one clear focal point, size it for the smallest screen it will appear on, keep one style across the whole blog, leave room for the headline, and test it on a phone before you publish. A good header does not shout. It supports the words, sets the mood, and earns the click in a single glance.

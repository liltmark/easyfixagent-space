AI Video Length Guide: How Long Should Each Type of Clip Be?

October 27, 2026 · Video · 6 min read

Length is one of the first decisions in any video project, and it shapes everything else: the script, the pacing, and whether viewers stay to the end. There is no single ideal length, but there are sensible ranges for each type of clip, and this guide lays them out.

1. The Golden Rule: As Long as the Idea Needs

The best length for a video is the shortest length that fully delivers its idea, and no longer. Padding a thirty-second idea into two minutes does not make it more valuable; it makes viewers leave, and platforms notice when they do. Before generating anything, write the core message in one sentence, then ask how many seconds that sentence honestly needs on screen. If the answer is fifteen seconds, do not stretch it, and if it needs three minutes, do not crush it into thirty. Respect for the viewer's time is the real retention strategy.

2. Short-Form Social Clips: 15 to 60 Seconds

For TikTok, Reels and Shorts, the sweet spot is usually between fifteen and sixty seconds, with the strongest hook in the first two seconds. These platforms reward completion, so a tight thirty-second clip that viewers finish will outperform a rambling ninety-second clip that they abandon halfway. One idea per clip works best: one tip, one joke, one before-and-after, one surprising fact. If your idea needs more room, split it into a series rather than a single long post, since each part can find its own audience and the series keeps viewers coming back.

3. Explainers and How-Tos: 2 to 5 Minutes

When viewers are actively trying to learn something, they will stay longer, and two to five minutes is a practical range for most explainers and how-to videos. This gives room for the problem, the steps in order, and a quick recap, without turning into a lecture. Structure matters more than length here: clear chapters, on-screen steps, and a pace that matches the difficulty of the task. If a tutorial genuinely needs ten minutes, that is fine, but break it into labelled sections so viewers can jump to the step they need, because most will not watch it straight through.

4. Ads and Promos: 6 to 30 Seconds

Advertising rewards brevity more than any other format. Six-second bumpers work for a single memorable message, fifteen seconds suits most social ads, and thirty seconds is the upper limit for a standard promo before attention drops sharply. Put the product and the benefit in the first three seconds, because many viewers skip as soon as they can, and end with one clear action rather than a list of options. 5. Product Demos: 60 to 90 Seconds

A product demo needs enough time to show the product working in a real situation, and sixty to ninety seconds usually covers it: the problem, the product in action, the result, and the next step. Shorter than a minute often feels rushed, with features flashed past too quickly to register, while longer than two minutes starts to feel like a sales pitch rather than a demonstration. Focus on one or two benefits rather than every feature, and show rather than describe wherever possible, since viewers remember what they saw working far better than what they heard listed.

6. Testimonials and Stories: 30 to 90 Seconds

Stories and testimonials run on emotion, and emotion fades if the clip drags. Thirty to ninety seconds is enough for a genuine-sounding story: the situation before, the turning point, and the result after. Longer testimonials tend to repeat themselves, and repetition is what makes viewers suspicious rather than convinced. Let the speaker sound natural, keep the editing simple, and resist adding a long introduction that delays the human voice viewers came to hear. If you have several stories, publish them separately instead of stitching them into one long compilation.

7. Long-Form and YouTube Videos: 8 to 15 Minutes

For standard YouTube content, eight to fifteen minutes remains a reliable range: long enough to cover a topic properly and to qualify for mid-roll advertising, but short enough to hold attention with good pacing. The key is that every minute must earn its place, with a clear structure, regular changes of visual, and no filler segments that exist only to reach a length target. If your topic needs twenty minutes, make twenty minutes, but be honest about whether it does.

8. How to Choose for Your Own Project

Start from the platform, then the purpose, then the idea. Check what length that platform's audience expects, confirm what your video must achieve, and then cut the script until only the essential beats remain. Generate a rough version, watch it once as a stranger, and note the exact second your attention wanders: that is where the cut belongs. Length is not a target to hit but a result of good editing, and the clips that perform best are almost always the ones where the creator was brave enough to remove something good to protect something great.

Bottom line: Match length to purpose — seconds for ads, a minute for social clips, a few minutes for explainers — and cut anything that does not earn its seconds.

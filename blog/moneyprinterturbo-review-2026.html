MoneyPrinterTurbo Review 2026: Free AI Video Automation, Tested

Written & tested by Muhammad Mudassir YaseenFounder, EasyFix Agent — every guide on this site is tested by hand before it is published.

Most "AI video generators" make one clip at a time in a browser tab. MoneyPrinterTurbo does something different: it runs on your own computer and automates the whole chain — script, footage, voiceover, subtitles, music, finished video — from a single topic line. We installed it, ran it end to end, and made a real short for exactly $0. This review is what we found, including the places where "free" quietly stops being free.

Quick verdict

MoneyPrinterTurbo is the most complete free video-automation tool we have tested. In our hands-on test it produced a finished 30.5-second vertical short — our own script, a local voiceover, word-perfect subtitles and background music — with no subscription, no watermark and no per-video fee. The trade-offs are real: you install and run it yourself, the free stock footage needs free API keys from stock sites, and a few tempting switches inside it (AI-generated footage, auto-uploading) lead straight to paid services. Set it up the free way, as we show below, and it is a genuine production tool — not a demo.

What MoneyPrinterTurbo is

MoneyPrinterTurbo is a free, open-source program (MIT licence) maintained in the open on GitHub at github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo, where it has gathered roughly 137,000 stars — an unusually large community for a tool of this kind, and a good sign for long-term maintenance. You run it on your own machine (Windows, macOS or Linux) through a simple web interface in your browser, a command line, or its API. Because it is local software rather than a hosted service, there is no account, no credit system and no watermark: the videos it renders are ordinary MP4 files on your own disk, in 9:16 for Shorts and TikTok, 16:9 for YouTube, or 1:1 for feeds. It also has a batch mode that queues several topics and renders them one after another — the feature that turns it from a toy into a small production line.

How the pipeline works

Give it a topic — say, "why is my AC blowing warm air" — and the chain runs in six steps:

  • Script. A language model writes a short narration script from your topic (or you paste your own script and skip the model entirely — our preferred route, explained below).
  • Footage. It searches free stock libraries for clips matching the script's keywords and downloads the best fits.
  • Voiceover. The script is read aloud by a text-to-speech voice; the free route is Microsoft's Edge TTS, with fully local voices as an alternative.
  • Subtitles. The audio is transcribed and the captions are timed word by word and burned into the video.
  • Music. A background track from the bundled library is mixed under the voice at a sensible level.
  • Render. FFmpeg assembles everything into the finished MP4 in your chosen format.

Every step has settings — voice, subtitle position and styling, music volume, clip length — but the defaults are sane, and a first video needs no tweaking at all.

Our hands-on test

We installed MoneyPrinterTurbo on a standard Linux machine and ran a complete job the way a beginner would, changing only what the free route requires. Our test video: a 30.5-second vertical (1080×1920) short on "AC blowing warm air — three causes every homeowner should check", the kind of practical short a local service business posts every day. We wrote the script ourselves and pasted it in, used a fully local voice (Piper) for the narration, let the built-in transcription create the subtitles, and used one of the bundled music tracks. Total spend: $0 — no card, no credits, no trial.

What genuinely impressed us: the subtitles and the render. The word-timed captions matched the narration exactly — we checked line by line against our script and found no errors — and the final assembly (cuts on the voice, music ducked underneath, clean vertical framing) looked like something that came out of a paid editor. What needed honesty: the stock footage step needs free API keys. The tool pulls clips from Pexels, Pixabay or Coverr, and each of those asks you to create a free account and copy a key into the settings. It takes a few minutes per site, costs nothing, and only needs doing once — but "download and it just works" is not quite true until those keys are in. And one environment note from our test: the free Edge TTS voice service was unreachable from our test network, so we switched the narration to a local Piper voice, which the tool supports through its custom-voice options. On a typical home connection Edge TTS works directly; either way, a free voice route exists and our finished video proves it.

Getting started, step by step

Here is the exact free setup we used, in plain order:

  • Step 1 — Install Python 3.11 or newer from python.org if you do not already have it, and make sure FFmpeg is installed (the project documentation lists it as a requirement; most systems can add it with one command).
  • Step 2 — Download the project from its GitHub page (the green Code button → Download ZIP, or git clone if you use Git), and unpack it somewhere you will keep it — it stores its videos and settings in its own folder.
  • Step 3 — Install its requirements by running pip install -r requirements.txt inside the project folder. This is the slow step on a first run; it is a one-time download of the libraries it needs.
  • Step 4 — Start the web interface with the included launcher (on Windows, the provided start script; elsewhere, the documented streamlit command). It opens a control panel in your browser — everything from here is buttons and boxes, no code.
  • Step 5 — Set the free script route. In the settings, either choose a free model option (Pollinations, an OpenRouter free model, or a free-tier key from Groq or Gemini), or do what we do: write your scripts yourself and paste them in. A script you wrote beats a generated one for accuracy anyway — our beginner's guide to making AI videos free covers the writing side.
  • Step 6 — Add your free stock keys. Create free accounts at Pexels and Pixabay, copy each API key from their developer pages, and paste them into the tool's settings. Five minutes, once, and the footage step is unlocked forever.
  • Step 7 — Make your first video. Enter a topic or paste a script, pick 9:16, leave the voice and subtitles on their free defaults, and press generate. Watch the finished MP4 before posting — then post it manually, with your own title and description.

Local automation or a browser studio?

Readers often ask how this compares with our own free AI Video Studio, which runs entirely in the browser. They solve different halves of the problem. The studio generates original AI clips from a sentence or a photo — use it when you need footage that does not exist yet, like animating a photo — but each clip is short and you assemble the final video yourself. MoneyPrinterTurbo generates nothing new visually; it assembles stock footage, voice, captions and music into a finished video automatically. Many creators will use both: studio clips for the hero shots, the local pipeline for the daily captioned explainers. Zero cost either way — the choice is effort versus automation.

Who it is for — and who should skip it

  • For: creators posting daily Shorts, TikToks or Reels on a schedule; small businesses making regular tip-and-explainer videos; anyone whose bottleneck is editing time, not ideas; batch producers who want several topics rendered unattended.
  • Not for: anyone who needs original, cinematic AI footage (that is a generator's job, not an assembler's); anyone unwilling to spend 20–30 minutes on a one-time install; and anyone expecting it to run a channel untouched — scripts still need a human eye, and posting should stay manual.

FAQ

Is MoneyPrinterTurbo really free? Yes — the program itself is free and open source, and every step of the pipeline has a working free route, as our $0 test shows. The paid traps are specific optional switches (the default script provider, AI-generated footage sources, premium voices and the auto-upload add-on), all listed in the table above.

Do I need a powerful computer? No. The heavy intelligence runs through whichever script model you choose, and the assembly is ordinary video rendering. Our test ran on a standard machine; a 30-second short rendered in about two minutes.

Can I use the videos commercially? The tool's MIT licence places no restriction on your output. The footage is the part to check: stock from Pexels, Pixabay and Coverr is licensed for commercial use under those sites' free licences — keep to their terms, as with any stock.

Does it add a watermark? No. Because it runs locally, there is no platform in the middle to stamp your video. The output is a clean MP4.

Can it upload videos to my channels automatically? An add-on exists, but it is a paid extra and we recommend against it: upload manually, so every title, description and posting time stays your decision.

The verdict

MoneyPrinterTurbo earned its popularity honestly: it is the closest thing to a free, private, one-click video department that we have tested. Install it once, set the free routes we mapped above, write (or paste) good scripts, and it returns finished, captioned, music-backed shorts at a pace no manual editor matches — for $0 a video. Keep the paid switches off, keep posting by hand, and it belongs in every budget creator's toolkit. The project lives at github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo.
